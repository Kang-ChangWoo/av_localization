\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} We evaluate on three floorplan-localization benchmarks
that share one acoustic simulation and differ in what their cameras and meshes
allow (Tab.~\ref{tab:setup}). Every pose carries an RGB image and a room impulse
response from a six-microphone ring of radius $5$\,cm with a co-located source,
rendered at $48$\,kHz with reflection depth $50$ and diffraction to order $10$.
\textbf{Replica} has $17$ rooms split $11/3/3$ and two motion collections over
each room. \textbf{Matterport3D} has $159$ rooms split $131/16/12$ with the same
two collections. \textbf{Structured3D} has $700$ houses split $400/50/250$, one
image per pose at the houses' own fixed camera positions, and no furnished
mesh. Candidate acoustics are rendered on the extruded floorplan in every case.
Where a furnished scan exists the query is rendered on it, so the query carries
furniture the candidates do not; that gap is the problem this paper addresses.
Structured3D has no such scan, so its query is rendered on the same extrusion as
its candidates, which removes the gap and makes the acoustic side a different,
easier problem. The main table names the query geometry on every row so the two
settings cannot be read as one.

\paragraph{Protocol.} The visual backbones are trained on the training rooms
of each benchmark and are never modified afterwards. The fusion has one shared
structure, chosen once: a hypothesis is summarised by the visual log-posterior
at its centre and by the $0.9$ quantile of the acoustic score over its $0.5$\,m
disc, combined by the gate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma}. Three scalars are
tuned per backbone, because visual posteriors from different networks sit on
different scales and cannot share a threshold on ambiguity.

Those scalars are chosen with \emph{rooms} held out. On Replica the two motion
collections are pooled and the scalars are refit on two rooms and reported on
the third, rotating; on Matterport3D and Structured3D alternate rooms are held
out. An earlier version of this work tuned on the forward-motion collection and
reported on the general-motion one. That is not a holdout for an acoustic term:
$73\%$ of general poses have a forward pose within one grid cell and $100\%$
within the $1$\,m threshold, and the acoustic score depends on position alone
(Sec.~\ref{sec:supp-split}). Moving to room holdout lowered every Replica gain
by roughly half, and the numbers here are the lower ones.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Recall at $0.1$, $0.5$, $1$, $2$ and $5$\,m and the joint
$1$\,m\,/\,$30^\circ$ recall, with median error, under the definitions of
\cite{f3loc} and the thresholds of \cite{unloc}. Gains carry $95\%$ paired
bootstrap intervals over queries. Because queries in one room share a floorplan
and an acoustic field, we also report room-clustered intervals
(Tab.~\ref{tab:cluster}); with three Replica rooms those span zero, and we do
not claim building-level generalisation from Replica alone. Every backbone and
the projection are trained once, with seed $0$; no number in this paper is an
average over training seeds, and the intervals measure query sampling only.

\subsection{Baselines are reproduced}
\label{sec:repro}

Since the contribution is a delta on top of existing methods, Tab.~\ref{tab:repro}
reproduces the published Gibson results of F3Loc, UnLoc and DisCo-FLoc from
their released weights under their own protocols. F3Loc and UnLoc match to
within $0.1$ point. DisCo-FLoc's RRP-only row is $1.0$ low and its published
value falls between the two sampling settings its code exposes, so we use
DisCo-FLoc's ray predictor as a backbone but do not extend its contrastive
stage, which loses $5.4$ points when transferred zero-shot. One detail matters
for anyone reproducing F3Loc: its evaluation leaves the monocular and
multi-view networks in training mode, so BatchNorm uses batch statistics and
the monocular network reads $46.8\%$ at batch $16$ against $36.5\%$ at batch
$1$. Every number here is at batch $1$.

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:main}

\input{tables/tab_main_roomcv}
\input{tables/tab_main_multi}

Tab.~\ref{tab:main_roomcv} is the headline. With rooms held out and the
structure fixed, attaching the acoustic term improves recall at $1$\,m by
$5.0$ points $[1.7, 8.5]$ on F3Loc and $7.0$ $[3.8, 10.2]$ on UnLoc, on $600$
queries each; on DisCo-FLoc it is $0.7$ $[-1.8, 3.2]$, which is no gain.
Median error falls from $2.15$ to $1.49$\,m on F3Loc and $0.90$ to $0.46$\,m on
UnLoc. The gain lives between $0.5$ and $2$\,m: at $0.1$\,m it is within
noise, because selecting among hypotheses cannot refine a position inside one,
and at $5$\,m the backbones are near saturation.

The structure is fixed on purpose. Searching it inside each fold as well, over
twelve summary pairs, moves F3Loc to $+4.2$, UnLoc to $+4.5$ and DisCo-FLoc to
$+2.7$, and picks a different pair in nearly every fold: with two fitting rooms
the search overfits them and the held-out room pays. A protocol whose result
depends on which of two rooms it was fitted on is reporting noise, so the
paper commits to one structure and lets the scalars alone move.

Tab.~\ref{tab:main_multi} adds the other two benchmarks. On Matterport3D, the
only other benchmark with a furnished query, the gains are $2.3$ $[-0.6, 5.2]$,
$3.3$ $[0.8, 5.8]$ and $2.3$ $[-0.8, 5.4]$ over $480$ queries in twelve rooms.
They are smaller than Replica's and only UnLoc's interval excludes zero.
Matterport3D rooms are larger, with a median of $13{,}186$ candidate cells
against Replica's $5{,}344$, and the furniture gap is wider: the acoustic score
alone reaches $4.2\%$ there against Replica's $20.7\%$. \textbf{The effect size
this paper claims is two to seven points on two backbones, and none on the
third.} It is small, and it is not universal: DisCo-FLoc's ray predictor gains
nothing on Replica under the fixed structure.

Structured3D gives $+23.6$, $+31.2$ and $+26.7$. We report these because it is
the only benchmark with thirty test rooms, which is what makes its intervals
tight, and we do not compare them with the furnished rows: with query and
candidate on the same geometry the acoustic score alone reaches $65.6\%$, and
the same rules that need a gate under a furnished query are better off without
one there (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}). Structured3D is the matched-geometry upper
bound of this method, not its result.

\subsection{What the acoustic term is doing}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/C_candidate_set}
\caption{Recall at $1$\,m against the size of the set the acoustic score is
asked to choose from, on Replica with rooms held out. Grey: the acoustic score
choosing alone. Blue: the gated fusion over the same hypotheses. Dashed: an
oracle over the same set. The acoustic score alone gets \emph{worse} as the
set grows on every backbone; gated by visual ambiguity it gets better.}
\label{fig:candidate}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Acoustics cannot search; it can only help choose.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:candidate} varies only the set of places the acoustic score is
asked to choose between. Over the whole grid it reaches $20.8\%$, which is the
global-localization number a reviewer expects from a floorplan-rendered model
against a furnished recording. Restricting it to the ten spatially separated
visual hypotheses does not rescue it: the acoustic score choosing alone among
them falls from $38.3\%$ at $K{=}1$ to $26.3\%$ at $K{=}10$ on F3Loc, and
likewise on the other two backbones. The gated fusion over the same ten
hypotheses, scored with rooms held out exactly as in Tab.~\ref{tab:main_roomcv},
rises instead, to $43.3\%$ on F3Loc and $57.7\%$ on UnLoc; on DisCo-FLoc it
reaches $41.2\%$, which is where the headline table leaves it. The claim is
therefore not that acoustics can choose among a few places, but that it can
help vision choose among a few places, and only where the visual posterior is
ambiguous. The oracle over the same ten hypotheses reaches $85$--$93\%$; the
distance to it is what better acoustic discrimination could still buy.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/I_pairwise}
\caption{Forced binary choice between the correct hypothesis and one incorrect
one, on Replica. Chance is $50\%$. The acoustic score is flat across strata;
the visual ordering is strong where vision is confident and weak where it is
ambiguous. The two cross exactly where the gate opens.}
\label{fig:pairwise}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The evidence is there even where the gain is small.} A $21\%$
acoustic-alone recall invites the objection that the cue is nearly empty.
Fig.~\ref{fig:pairwise} answers it with a forced binary choice between the
correct hypothesis and one incorrect one, $4{,}500$--$5{,}000$ pairs per
backbone. The acoustic score picks correctly $76$--$80\%$ of the time and is
within three points of that in every stratum, visually ambiguous or confident,
spatially close or far. The visual ordering is not: it reaches $78$--$89\%$
where vision is confident and falls to $66$--$75\%$ where it is ambiguous. In
the ambiguous stratum the acoustic score beats the visual ordering by $10.7$,
$5.3$ and $9.7$ points on the three backbones; in the confident stratum vision
wins. That crossover is the empirical justification for gating on visual
ambiguity rather than on anything acoustic.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/V_visual_degradation}
\caption{Recall at $1$\,m as the query image is corrupted before the frozen
F3Loc backbone, Replica with rooms held out, scalars refit per level. The
acoustic side is untouched. The gain widens as vision fails, and the gate opens
on every query; but the fusion stays below the acoustic score alone once the
visual shortlist it verifies is itself ruined.}
\label{fig:degrade}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The gain grows as the camera degrades, up to a ceiling.} If the
mechanism is that acoustics intervenes where vision is unsure, then corrupting
the query image should lower vision, leave the acoustic side untouched, and
widen the gap. Fig.~\ref{fig:degrade} shows that it does. A Gaussian blur of
$\sigma{=}2$\,px, mild enough to be hard to see, drops F3Loc's recall at $1$\,m
from $38.3\%$ to $5.5\%$ and raises the acoustic gain from $+5.0$ $[1.7, 8.3]$
to $+7.3$ $[4.5, 10.2]$; dimming the image to a quarter of its intensity drops
vision to $5.3\%$ and raises the gain to $+11.7$ $[8.7, 14.7]$, with the gate
now opening on every query. The acoustic term engages by itself when the camera
fails, which is the behaviour a deployment wants.

Two caveats belong beside that. First, a backbone trained only on clean renders
is brittle to a degree the paper should not hide, and the acoustic gain is the
smaller of the two facts. Second, the fusion under heavy degradation, at
$12$--$17\%$, sits \emph{below} the acoustic score alone over the whole grid at
$20.8\%$. The method verifies a visual shortlist; when the shortlist itself no
longer contains the answer, verifying it cannot recover what a global acoustic
search would, and a deployment that can detect a dead camera should fall back
to that search rather than to this fusion.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}

\input{tables/tab_fusion}
\input{tables/tab_feature}

\paragraph{Fusion rule.} Tab.~\ref{tab:fusion} varies only the combination
rule, on the motion-collection split so that the sweep is affordable. Three
rows matter. Unconditional reranking among hypotheses loses $15.7$ points: sound
cannot be trusted to choose by itself, which Fig.~\ref{fig:candidate} shows at
every $K$. Both cell-wise rules have intervals that span zero. Replacing the
smooth gate by a hard threshold turns the result negative, because near either
boundary a hard rule commits fully on an arbitrarily small difference.

The same table under matched geometry inverts the ordering: unconditional
reranking becomes the best rule and every gate costs points. The gating
machinery is compensating for the furniture gap and would be harmful without
it, which is the more useful sentence than ``our gate is best''.

\paragraph{Acoustic feature.} Tab.~\ref{tab:feature} shows a disagreement
between two criteria. Ordered by how well sound localises alone, a $2$\,ms
envelope is best; ordered by how often the feature picks the correct
hypothesis, a banded spectrogram at $5.33$\,ms leads by $6.0$ points. The
second is the task, and selecting on the fit rooms alone gives the same winner.

\paragraph{Multi-view vision.} A vision reviewer asks whether the ambiguity is
simply a field-of-view problem that four views would remove. F3Loc's
multi-view and complementary networks are trained on Replica and Matterport3D,
so we attached the acoustic term to them. On Replica the answer is not
available: the official evaluator puts the monocular, multi-view and
complementary networks at $35.0$, $33.9$ and $34.6\%$, because a $0.27$\,m
chunk step gives multi-view stereo almost no baseline, and on the
general-motion collection, a third of which is in-place rotation, the
multi-view network falls to $20.6\%$. Over both collections the acoustic gain
is $+2.5$ $[-1.2, 6.2]$ on the complementary network and $-2.2$ $[-6.0, 1.7]$
on multi-view. The negative sign is informative: the gate reads the multi-view
network's near-uniform posterior on rotation chunks as ambiguity and lets
acoustics act, but that posterior is uninformative rather than undecided. A
gate on visual ambiguity cannot distinguish the two, and the paper does not
claim that it can. Whether the gain survives a genuinely stronger backbone is
a question Matterport3D can answer and Replica cannot; it is left for the
supplementary.

\subsection{What limits the method}
\label{sec:bounds}

\input{tables/tab_bounds}

Tab.~\ref{tab:bounds} bounds the problem from two sides. An oracle over the
ten hypotheses reaches $93.7\%$ against the method's $54.7\%$ on the
collection split, so the shortlist is not the constraint. Replacing the
furnished query with one rendered on the same wall-only mesh as the candidates,
changing nothing else, moves the acoustic score alone from $20.7\%$ to
$94.3\%$ and the median ground-truth rank from $192$ to $1$. The furniture
domain gap, not the fusion, is what limits the method, and Matterport3D
reproduces the same picture at $4.2\%$ against $81.9\%$.

We tried to close the gap without furniture and report both attempts as
negative. A per-component reliability weight estimated on the training rooms
correlates $0.04$ and $-0.05$ with the same quantity on two test rooms. A
paired regression from the furnished to the wall-only domain improves its own
held-out loss threefold and leaves the median ground-truth rank unchanged: it
learns the average effect of furniture, which is common to every candidate and
cancels in a ranking.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{The effect is small.} Two to four points at $1$\,m on furnished
queries, with room-clustered intervals that graze zero on Replica's three rooms.
We regard the characterisation of when an approximate acoustic forward model
is and is not useful as the contribution, and the localization gain as one
consequence of it.

\textbf{Both acoustic domains are simulated.} The furnished and wall-only
conditions differ only in the mesh handed to one engine. Nothing here shows a
real microphone behaves this way; a real-room measurement is the necessary next
experiment and its design is in the supplementary.

\textbf{Sound supplies no heading.} The ring's channels are interchangeable at
a $5$\,cm aperture (Tab.~\ref{tab:ringrot}), so every orientation reported is
the visual backbone's. A binaural pair does not change this: with a source
co-located with the listener there is no direction of arrival for a head-related
transfer function to resolve, and the best of $36$ headings at the true cell
lands within $30^\circ$ at the chance rate (Sec.~\ref{sec:supp-ring}).

\textbf{The candidate grid is an offline cost}, about four seconds of
single-core simulation per cell, or roughly five core-hours for a
$5{,}000$-cell floorplan, paid once per building.

\textbf{The gain does not reach fine localization or a beyond-field-of-view
claim.} At $0.1$\,m the method is within noise of the backbone by construction.
Splitting the response by arrival time places the late range below chance at
ranking the true pose, so we make no claim that the gain comes from unseen
geometry; vision and acoustics have different spatial support, and that is the
motivation we state.
