\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem setting}
\label{sec:setup}

A floorplan is pre-processed, following \cite{f3loc}, into a directional
Euclidean signed distance field $D \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times O}$ on a
$0.1$\,m grid with $O{=}36$ yaw bins, where $D[y,x,o]$ is the free distance from
cell $(x,y)$ along bin $o$. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the navigable cells. A visual
backbone maps one image to $V$ horizontal depth rays
$\hat{\mathbf{r}} \in \mathbb{R}^{V}$ and induces the pose posterior
%
\begin{equation}
  p_v(y,x,o) \;\propto\; \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{1}{\lambda}
  \big\| D[y,x,o{:}o{+}V] - \hat{\mathbf{r}} \big\|_1 \Big),
  \label{eq:visual}
\end{equation}
%
with $\lambda{=}40$. We write $\pi(c) = \max_o p_v(c,o)$ and
$\omega(c) = \arg\max_o p_v(c,o)$. We treat \eqref{eq:visual} as a black box:
the backbone is never retrained or modified, and we report two backbones that
differ in how $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ is produced.\footnote{One of them additionally
predicts a per-ray Laplace scale and divides the residual in \eqref{eq:visual}
by it. Nothing below depends on that.}

At the same pose, a co-located loudspeaker and a six-microphone ring record a
room impulse response $g \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times N}$. A candidate response
$g_c$ is rendered offline at every $c \in \mathcal{C}$ from the floorplan
geometry alone. The asymmetry is the constraint the method is built around: the
query is what a microphone in a furnished room hears, while every candidate is
what bare walls produce, because bare walls are all a floorplan specifies.

\subsection{Why fusion at the level of hypotheses}
\label{sec:why}

The obvious construction combines the two scores at every cell. We find it
degenerate. Ranking $\pi$ over the $\sim\!5{,}000$ navigable cells and taking
logarithms, the visual term spans $0.009$ across the fifty cells the backbone
shortlists, since all of them lie above the $99.9$th percentile, while the
acoustic term spans $0.67$: a factor of $72$. A cell-wise weighted sum is
therefore not a weighted sum. The visual posterior does not participate, and the
acoustic score decides alone among the shortlist. Over $300$ queries it moves
the prediction on $92\%$ of them and improves it on $52\%$, a coin flip
(Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}).

The shortlist is also miscounted. Vision's top fifty cells are typically two or
three distinct \emph{places}, each sampled many times, so a broad mode outvotes
a sharp one by cell count alone. Ten separated places attain the ground-truth
coverage of fifty cells, $93.7\%$ against $93.0\%$, with a fifth of the
candidates (Tab.~\ref{tab:bounds}).

Both observations point the same way: the unit of fusion should be a place, not
a cell.

\subsection{Spatial hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

We extract $K{=}10$ separated modes of $\pi$ by greedy non-maximum suppression,
%
\begin{equation}
  h_k = \!\!\arg\max_{c \,\in\, \mathcal{C} \setminus \bigcup_{j<k} B(h_j)}\!\! \pi(c),
  \quad
  B(h) = \{ c : \| c - h \|_2 \le \rho_{\mathrm{nms}} \},
  \label{eq:nms}
\end{equation}
%
with $\rho_{\mathrm{nms}}{=}1.5$\,m, and write
$\mathcal{D}_k = \{c : \|c - h_k\|_2 \le \rho_{\mathrm{loc}}\}$ for a disc of
radius $\rho_{\mathrm{loc}}{=}0.5$\,m about each hypothesis. The suppression
radius is also what defines visual confidence in Sec.~\ref{sec:gate}, so
extraction and confidence stay consistent.

The method only ever \emph{selects} among $\{h_1,\dots,h_K\}$; the prediction is
$(h_{k^\star},\, \omega(h_{k^\star}))$. Position and yaw both come from the
visual backbone at the selected hypothesis. Sound cannot move the answer to a
place vision did not propose, and cannot alter yaw, about which a co-located
source and receiver carry no information. Consequently our joint
position-and-orientation numbers measure landing on a better cell, never a
better heading.

\subsection{Acoustic representation}
\label{sec:acoustic}

Each response is aligned on its direct arrival; the first $2$\,ms are discarded,
so the direct sound and any constant engine gain cancel between query and
candidate, and the following $128$\,ms are analysed. We compute a short-time
spectrogram with an $n_{\mathrm{fft}}{=}256$ Hann window and hop $64$ at
$48$\,kHz, a $5.33$\,ms analysis window at $187$\,Hz resolution, and sum power
into three bands, $[0,0.5)$, $[0.5,1.5)$ and $[1.5,4)$\,kHz, giving
$\phi(g) \in \mathbb{R}^{18 \times T}$. Each row is normalised to unit sum and
concatenated with the per-row energy split, making $\phi$ invariant to level.
The acoustic score of a cell is
%
\begin{equation}
  a(c) \;=\; -\big\| \phi(g_c) - \phi(g) \big\|_1 .
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}

The analysis window is not the conventional choice, and the reason is worth
stating because it generalises. Ranking features by how well sound localises
\emph{alone} selects a $2$\,ms energy envelope. Ranking them by how often they
identify the correct hypothesis, which is the only thing \eqref{eq:evidence}
asks, selects the spectrogram above, by $6.0$ points. The two criteria disagree
(Tab.~\ref{tab:feature}), and a feature chosen for a task the method does not
perform is a feature chosen wrongly.

\subsection{Relative acoustic evidence}
\label{sec:evidence}

Sound is a weak absolute localiser in this setting: alone it reaches $20.7\%$ at
$1$\,m against $49.7\%$ for the stronger visual backbone. We therefore never ask
it where the query is, only which proposed place is most consistent with what
was heard.

Each hypothesis is summarised by a robust aggregate over its disc, the $0.9$
quantile $\alpha_k = Q_{0.9}\{a(c) : c \in \mathcal{D}_k\}$, rather than by its
centre. Measurement motivates this: the ground-truth cell has median acoustic
rank $0.950$, while the best cell within $0.25$\,m of it has $0.992$. The score
identifies the right region far more reliably than the right cell.

Since \eqref{eq:score} is unnormalised with a query-dependent offset, we
standardise across the hypotheses of that query,
$\tilde{\alpha}_k = (\alpha_k - \mu_\alpha)/\sigma_\alpha$, and define
%
\begin{equation}
  \varrho_k \;=\; \tilde{\alpha}_k \;-\; \log \!\!\sum_{j \neq k}\!
  \exp \tilde{\alpha}_j .
  \label{eq:evidence}
\end{equation}
%
Unlike a global percentile, \eqref{eq:evidence} is invariant to a constant shift
of the score and states the comparative question directly.

\subsection{Confidence-gated selection}
\label{sec:gate}

Sound must not overrule a confident visual prediction. Visual ambiguity is the
log-odds between the two strongest hypotheses and acoustic discriminativeness is
the margin of \eqref{eq:evidence},
%
\begin{equation}
  m_v = \log \pi(h_{1}) - \log \pi(h_{2}),
  \qquad
  m_a = \varrho_{(1)} - \varrho_{(2)},
  \label{eq:margins}
\end{equation}
%
with $\varrho_{(i)}$ the $i$-th largest evidence. Both are computable at
inference and neither uses ground truth. The acoustic term enters through a
product of two smooth gates,
%
\begin{equation}
  \gamma = w \,\cdot\,
  \sigma\!\Big(\tfrac{\tau_v - m_v}{s}\Big) \cdot
  \sigma\!\Big(\tfrac{m_a - \tau_a}{s}\Big),
  \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
%
and the hypothesis is selected by
%
\begin{equation}
  k^\star = \arg\max_k \;\; \tilde{v}_k + \gamma \, \varrho_k ,
  \qquad
  \tilde{v}_k = \frac{\log \pi(h_k) - \mu_v}{\sigma_v}.
  \label{eq:select}
\end{equation}
%
Standardising both terms per query is what makes $w$, $\tau_v$ and $\tau_a$
transferable rather than scale-dependent. Setting $\gamma{=}0$ recovers the
visual backbone exactly, so the method contains its own baseline as a special
case and cannot be credited for a gain the baseline already has.

Smoothness is not cosmetic. Replacing \eqref{eq:gamma} by a hard threshold costs
$9.3$ points and turns the gain negative (Tab.~\ref{tab:fusion}): near either
boundary a hard rule commits fully on an arbitrarily small difference in
$m_v$ or $m_a$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Acoustic hypothesis verification}
\label{alg:method}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require image $I$, response $g$, DESDF $D$, candidate grid $\{g_c\}$
\State $p_v \gets$ \eqref{eq:visual} from the visual backbone
\State $\pi(c) \gets \max_o p_v(c,o)$; \; $\omega(c) \gets \arg\max_o p_v(c,o)$
\State $\{h_k\}_{k=1}^{K} \gets$ NMS on $\pi$ at $\rho_{\mathrm{nms}}$
       \Comment{\eqref{eq:nms}}
\State $a(c) \gets -\|\phi(g_c) - \phi(g)\|_1$ for $c \in \bigcup_k \mathcal{D}_k$
\State $\alpha_k \gets Q_{0.9}\{a(c) : c \in \mathcal{D}_k\}$
\State $\varrho \gets$ \eqref{eq:evidence} on standardised $\alpha$
\State $\gamma \gets$ \eqref{eq:gamma} from $m_v, m_a$
\State $k^\star \gets \arg\max_k \tilde{v}_k + \gamma \varrho_k$
\State \Return $(h_{k^\star},\, \omega(h_{k^\star}))$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{What is shared and what is tuned}
\label{sec:impl}

The structural choices, namely the summaries that represent a hypothesis under
each modality and the form of \eqref{eq:select}, are selected once and shared by
every backbone; letting them differ would describe two methods as one. The four
scalars $(w, s, \tau_v, \tau_a)$ are tuned per backbone, which is necessary
because two posteriors on different scales cannot share a threshold on $m_v$.
Sharing the structure costs about one point per backbone relative to fully
per-backbone tuning, and we report the shared configuration.

All selection, the acoustic feature of Sec.~\ref{sec:acoustic} included, uses
one pose collection; every reported number comes from a disjoint one
(Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}).

Rendering the candidate grid is the dominant cost and is done once per floorplan,
offline: $2{,}700$ to $5{,}400$ cells per scene. At inference the acoustic branch
is one spectrogram and $K$ disc aggregations, negligible beside the backbone.
